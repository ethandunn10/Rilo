Why does your app need Family Controls (Screen Time) access?

Rilo is a screen time app that helps people cut back on the apps they lose time to, like TikTok or Instagram. It is for the person using the iPhone to manage their own screen time, so it asks for individual authorization (.individual). It is not a parental control app and does not monitor anyone else.

Rilo uses the Screen Time APIs for these things:

1. FamilyControls: the person picks the apps they want help with in Apple's own app picker (FamilyActivityPicker). Rilo only gets Apple's tokens for those apps. It never sees the app names, the bundle IDs, or anything else on the phone.

2. ManagedSettings: Rilo puts a shield on the picked apps. Each app gets a few "opens" a day, and each open lasts a set number of minutes. When an app is opened, the shield asks what it's being opened for, then lets it through for that session. When the opens run out, the app stays shielded until midnight. People can also block an app fully, or block apps during bedtime, school hours or a focus block they set. An optional strict mode turns on "deny app removal", so the person can't just delete an app to get around their own limits.

3. DeviceActivity: Rilo schedules a daily reset at midnight (fresh opens), and it ends a session when its minutes are used up so the shield goes back on.

4. Shield configuration and shield action extensions: these show Rilo's own message on the shield screen, with a "talk to rilo" button.

Everything stays on the iPhone. Rilo has no accounts and no server. No Screen Time data, app list or usage is sent anywhere, shared or used for ads.
